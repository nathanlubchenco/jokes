# Prompt Engineering for Comedy

## The Comedy Prompt Framework

### Template Structure:
```
[PERSPECTIVE] + [OBSERVATION] + [TECHNIQUE] + [CONSTRAINTS] + [STRUCTURE]
```

## Example Prompts (Bad → Good)

### Example 1: Technology

❌ **Bad**: "Tell me a joke about smartphones"

⚠️ **Mediocre**: "Write a funny observation about how people use smartphones"

✅ **Good**: "You're a standup comedian who noticed that we now FaceTime elderly relatives, forcing them to stare directly at their own aging face while talking. Find the dark comedy in this. Use misdirection - start with seeming wholesome, end with an uncomfortable truth. 60 seconds, include a tag."

🏆 **Excellent**: "Adopt the perspective of someone who just realized: We convinced grandparents that to talk to family, they need to hold a camera 8 inches from their face at the worst possible angle, then WATCH THEMSELVES talk in a tiny mirror. This is some Black Mirror shit we've normalized. Build a bit that starts with 'staying connected is beautiful' and ends revealing how dystopian it actually is. Include a specific moment when you realized this (make it detailed and real). Use the Rule of Three for the aging relatives' reactions. Avoid obvious 'old people don't understand tech' - go weirder."

---

### Example 2: Relationships

❌ **Bad**: "Joke about dating"

⚠️ **Mediocre**: "Write something funny about online dating"

✅ **Good**: "You're doing observational comedy about how dating apps give you a 'personality preview' through photos, but we're all terrible at showing our actual personality. Find a specific example of a photo that seems normal but is actually insane. Make it a 45-second bit with a callback setup."

🏆 **Excellent**: "Build a bit around this observation: Dating app profiles are basically people saying 'I'm an adventure-seeker!' then showing a photo from the ONE time they went outside in 2019. You're the comedian who kept receipts - you matched with 'Adventure Sarah' whose entire personality is that she touched a mountain once. Develop this through escalating specificity. First tag: her other 'adventures'. Second tag: what her actual weekend looks like. Third tag: the reveal of YOUR profile lies. Make yourself the ultimate target. End with a callback setup about 'touching a mountain once.' Avoid sincerity - stay cynical but self-aware."

## Advanced Prompt Techniques

### 1. **Forced Perspective Shift**
"Write this from the perspective of [unexpected entity]"

Example:
- "You're the Uber driver watching couples have their breakup conversation in your backseat"
- "You're the phone watching someone doom-scroll at 2 AM"
- "You're the person who has to explain NFTs to their disappointed parents"

### 2. **The Constraint Method**
Force better material by forbidding the obvious

Example:
"Joke about therapy but you CANNOT mention:
- Couches
- 'How does that make you feel?'
- Childhood trauma
- Money/cost

Focus instead on the weird power dynamic of paying someone to care."

### 3. **The Escalation Ladder**
"Generate a joke at each level:"

```
Level 1: Relatable observation
Level 2: Specific detail that makes it weirder
Level 3: Dark implication
Level 4: Absurdist extrapolation
Level 5: Full dystopian nightmare / perfect callback
```

### 4. **The Technique Spec**
Name the exact comedy technique you want:

- "Use Mitch Hedberg-style literalism on the phrase 'work-life balance'"
- "Write a Jeselnik-style misdirection about meditation apps"
- "Create a Mulaney-esque story with at least 2 voices about getting locked out"
- "Channel Maria Bamford's specificity about your weirdest anxiety"

### 5. **The Against-The-Grain Prompt**
Take the expected angle and flip it:

"Everyone jokes that NYC is expensive and dirty. Instead, find something deeply weird about NYC that people DON'T talk about. Make it so specific that only someone who's lived there would notice."

### 6. **The Callback Architecture**
"Generate a 5-minute set where:
- Minute 1: Introduce concept A (seems like throwaway)
- Minute 2: Unrelated concept B
- Minute 3: Concept C that secretly relates to both
- Minute 4: First callback to A
- Minute 5: Tie all three together in a way that recontextualizes everything"

### 7. **The Reality Check Prompt**
"Write a bit, then critique it for:
- Predictability (rate 1-10)
- Specificity (is it specific to YOU?)
- Hack-iness (does it feel stolen?)
- Performability (can a human deliver this naturally?)
Then rewrite addressing the weaknesses."

## Topic-Specific Prompt Templates

### Modern Life:
```
"You're a comedian who just realized [SPECIFIC MODERN BEHAVIOR] is insane when you describe it out loud. Heighten through specificity - give me the exact moment, the exact phrase, the exact realization. Make yourself complicit (you do it too). End with how normalized it's become. Structure: Innocent intro → uncomfortable detail → implication → 'we all do this' solidarity → dark button."
```

### Personal Stories:
```
"Tell a story about [EVENT] but structure it like a heist movie - build tension, introduce 'characters' (give them specific traits), create a problem, escalate, fail in a specific way. Make the mundane feel epic, then undercut it. Your perspective: self-aware about how melodramatic you're being, but committed to the bit."
```

### Social Commentary:
```
"You notice [SOCIAL PHENOMENON]. Don't lecture - find the absurd logic that makes it make sense, then push that logic until it breaks. Start by steel-manning the position, then find the crack. Use a specific example. Make it about human nature, not politics. End with a turn that makes the audience laugh at themselves."
```

### Wordplay/Conceptual:
```
"Take the phrase [PHRASE] and find what's literally happening beneath the metaphor. Use Mitch Hedberg energy - deadpan observation of the literal interpretation leading to absurd conclusion. Keep it tight - 2 sentences max. Make the logic ironclad even as it's stupid."
```

## Prompts for Different Comedy Styles

### Dark Comedy:
"Address [TOPIC] but find the wrongness everyone's thinking but won't say. Start sympathetic, earn the trust, then go there. Justify why it's OK to laugh (benign violation). Make yourself the target too. End before it becomes mean."

### Absurdist:
"Take a normal thing [X] and follow its logic to a surreal conclusion. Each step must follow logically from the previous, but the endpoint is insane. Use deadpan delivery. Commit fully - no winking at the audience."

### Observational:
"You're hyper-aware of [SMALL THING NO ONE TALKS ABOUT]. Describe it with way too much specificity. Find the universal in the specific. Make people realize they DO know what you mean, they just never put it into words. Build to an 'oh my god, yes' realization."

### Self-Deprecating:
"Tell the story of [YOUR FAILURE] but frame it as competence in the wrong direction. You didn't fail randomly - you succeeded at being an idiot. Make it specific enough that it could only happen to you, relatable enough that we see ourselves. End with a callback-able phrase about your level of dysfunction."

### Surreal/Alternative:
"Present [NORMAL THING] as if you're an anthropologist explaining human behavior to aliens. Every assumption gets questioned. Use specificity to make the alien perspective feel real. End with a reveal that you DO understand but find it weird anyway."

## The Meta-Prompt for Joke Generation

```
I need a joke about [TOPIC].

Before you write, think through:
1. What's the OBVIOUS joke here? (Now avoid it)
2. What's MY unique perspective on this?
3. What's the specific moment/detail that makes this real?
4. What's the assumption I can weaponize?
5. Where's the misdirection opportunity?
6. What's uncomfortable but true here?
7. How can I make MYSELF the target?
8. What's the callback potential?

Now write the bit using:
- Technique: [SPECIFIC TECHNIQUE]
- Length: [TIME/WORD COUNT]
- Structure: [SETUP/TAG/CALLBACK etc]
- Tone: [DARK/ABSURD/OBSERVATIONAL etc]
- Constraint: DO NOT mention [OBVIOUS THINGS]

Then test it:
- Read it aloud - does it have rhythm?
- Is the punchline surprising but inevitable?
- Could you explain WHY it's funny? (If yes, rewrite)
- On a scale of 1-10, how specific is it?
- Would this work at an open mic?
```

## Iteration Prompts

### For Improving Weak Jokes:

"This joke isn't working: [JOKE]

Diagnose:
- Where's the exact moment it becomes predictable?
- What's the weakest word/phrase?
- Is the setup doing enough work?
- Is the punchline punching hard enough?
- What am I assuming the audience knows?

Rewrite three versions:
1. Same premise, different angle
2. Same punchline, better setup
3. Completely inverted - make the setup the punchline"

### For Developing Premises:

"Take this premise: [PREMISE]

Generate:
1. The obvious joke (so we can avoid it)
2. The dark version
3. The absurdist version
4. The self-deprecating version
5. The 'actually, the opposite is true' version

Pick the best and develop it fully with tags."

### For Building Callbacks:

"Review this set: [FULL SET]

Identify:
1. Which bit has the most callback potential?
2. What phrases/concepts appear multiple times?
3. Where can I plant a setup that pays off later?
4. What's the final callback that ties it together?

Rewrite the set with deliberate callback architecture."

## Common Prompt Mistakes to Avoid

❌ "Write a funny joke" - too vague
❌ "Make it hilarious" - not actionable
❌ "Be edgy" - recipe for try-hard nonsense
❌ "Like [COMEDIAN] would say it" - leads to ripoff energy
❌ No constraints - leads to first-draft thinking
❌ No structure specified - gets rambling
❌ No perspective defined - generic voice
❌ Asking for multiple unrelated jokes - no cohesion

## The Ultimate Joke Prompt Formula

```
[ROLE]: You're a standup comedian with [SPECIFIC PERSPECTIVE]

[OBSERVATION]: You've noticed [HYPER-SPECIFIC THING] about [TOPIC]

[TECHNIQUE]: Build this using [COMEDY TECHNIQUE]

[STRUCTURE]:
- Setup: [REQUIREMENT]
- Heightening: [REQUIREMENT]
- Punchline: [REQUIREMENT]
- Tag: [REQUIREMENT]

[CONSTRAINTS]:
- DO NOT mention: [OBVIOUS APPROACHES]
- Must include: [SPECIFIC DETAIL]
- Tone: [SPECIFIC TONE]
- Length: [WORD/TIME COUNT]

[TEST]:
After writing, verify:
- Surprise factor: [METRIC]
- Specificity: [METRIC]
- Performability: [METRIC]

If any test fails, rewrite that element.
```

Remember: **Specific prompts → Specific comedy → Better laughs**
